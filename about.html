About · The short version

The person behind the boxes.

I’m Zifeng — a graduate student, a detector-builder, and a person who thinks best with a notebook open.

I study computer vision and security at Beijing Institute of Technology, in the School of Cyberspace Science and Technology. My work asks how machines can find small things in large, messy scenes — and what it means to deploy that ability responsibly.

I started graduate school in 2023. Like a lot of first-year students, I began with Andrew Ng’s machine learning course and a notebook full of definitions. That notebook became VectorBlog, a place to record what I was learning about regression, clustering, and the privacy properties of blockchains. This site is the next version of that habit: slower, cleaner, and a little more honest about what I do and don’t know.

The name Vector comes from that first blog. I still like it. A vector has direction and magnitude; a good research question should have both.

These days my attention is split between detection models and the systems around them. I care about benchmarks, but I care more about what happens when a model leaves the dataset: different sensors, bad weather, edge hardware, and the social questions that arrive when a camera is pointed at a public sky.

This site is where I keep the working notes — the failed experiments, the diagrams that finally made sense, and the papers I wish someone had explained to me earlier.
