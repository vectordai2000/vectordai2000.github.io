About · The short version

The person behind the networks.

I’m Zifeng — a graduate student at Beijing Institute of Technology. I study how UAV-assisted edge systems coordinate, learn, and protect the data they depend on; I build the simulator and the learning algorithm, then write down what breaks.

UAV networks Multi-agent RL Federated learning Privacy Edge computing

I work at the intersection of networked systems and machine learning.

I design UAV-assisted edge environments and the multi-agent algorithms that operate in them, with a focus on the assumptions that make clean benchmarks fail: hybrid action spaces, long-term fairness, non-stationary peers, heterogeneous clients, and privacy noise.

Two questions organize most of what I do. First, how does a swarm coordinate when every agent is learning and the action space mixes continuous flight with discrete offloading? Second, how can clients learn together when their data is Non-IID and cannot leave the device? MAPA-MARL and PQ-FRL are my current answers; both are first-author papers, and both come with public code.

I came to research through writing. In 2023 I started graduate school and a notebook that became VectorBlog, where I worked through regression, clustering, and the privacy properties of blockchains. That habit stayed: I write to find the edges of what I understand. This site is the continuation — fewer announcements, more working notes.

I care about the parts that are easy to skip: a simulator that matches its stated assumptions, a baseline that is actually competitive, a failure case that is reported instead of averaged away. The notes here are where I keep those details, including the ones that did not fit in a paper.
